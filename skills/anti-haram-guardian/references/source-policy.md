# Source and ruling policy

## Purpose

This policy prevents confident religious misinformation. A citation is not decoration: it must support the exact proposition beside it.

## Evidence hierarchy

1. Qur'an: cite `surah:ayah` and link a stable verse page. Identify an English rendering as a translation of meaning, not the Arabic Qur'an itself.
2. Hadith: cite collection and canonical number. Prefer Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim; for other collections, report the displayed grading and do not silently strengthen it.
3. Scholarly application: use an official scholar, fatwa body, or academy page. Attribute the view to that source rather than calling it universal.
4. Classical works: cite edition, volume, and page only when verified from a reliable text. Do not recycle unverified page numbers.
5. Contemporary facts: use primary legal, financial, platform, or product documentation where possible.

## Claim labels

- `Direct text`: the verse or hadith states the operative principle.
- `Interpretation`: a scholar explains the text or connects it to a category.
- `Application`: a scholar or institution applies the principle to a modern medium or transaction.
- `Precautionary default`: this skill chooses the stricter practical route without claiming universal agreement.

## Research record

For a new source, capture:

```text
Topic:
Claim supported:
Evidence type: direct text | interpretation | application
Source authority:
Stable URL:
Reference number or date:
Disagreement or limitation:
Checked on:
```

Add only verified entries to `sources.csv`. Paraphrase unless a short quotation materially matters. Never quote a translation from memory.

## Disagreement discipline

When scholars differ:

1. Say the issue is disputed.
2. Describe the relevant positions neutrally and briefly.
3. State which cautious default the skill applies to its own output.
4. Do not declare adherents of another recognized view sinful.
5. Refer personal cases to a trusted, qualified scholar who can examine the facts.

## Prohibited shortcuts

- Search snippets as final proof.
- Unverified social posts or anonymous fatwa screenshots.
- A later scholar's grading presented as if it were part of the original collection.
- “All scholars agree” without a verified consensus source.
- A general verse used as if it directly names a modern technology.
- A policy preference mislabeled as a divine ruling.
